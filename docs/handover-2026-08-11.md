## Blocked on the human, and why

### #127 — duplicate order numbers. Two data decisions, deliberately deferred.

The code fix exists on `wave3/payment-seq` (`3eebdf8`, `d51dcee`; `d0cad95` is a mixed #127/#103
commit that would drag #103 along). It carries a migration that **cannot be applied**.

**Production has 282 duplicate `(firebase_restaurant_id, order_number)` pairs.** The unique index
cannot already exist — it would have prevented them. So the migration is *genuinely absent*, not
silently present; the `127/127` drift reading says nothing here, because the migration is not on
`main` and was never in the 127.

- **279 pairs are `restaurant_test_*` fixtures**, all inserted inside a 25-second window on
  2026-04-27T02:34, none with a `restaurants` row.
- **3 pairs are FNB ChowNow** (`b161c758-…`), a real production restaurant, newest 2026-07-24.

**The stated decisions, so they are not re-derived:**

> *"I'm not deciding the FNB ChowNow pairs tonight. Two real orders sharing a number is a receipt a
> customer already holds, and renumbering rewrites what they were told. That needs me awake. The
> 279 fixtures are probably deletable but they're production rows and I'm not signing that off at
> this hour either."*

**The data that decision needs** (all measured, on issue #127):

- **All six FNB orders are `paid` / `completed` / card.** Both orders in every pair were really
  paid.
- **Pairs 420 and 448 have different totals** — N$34 vs N$78, and N$26 vs N$46. These are two
  genuinely different bills sharing one number, not one order double-recorded. A refund against
  "order 420" is ambiguous between two amounts.
- **It is the `count(*)+1` race, unambiguously.** All 282 pairs are ≤1 second apart; zero are
  wider. FNB's three are 0.407s, 0.187s, 0.247s. The other failure mode the migration header
  describes — delete an order, the count drops below `max(order_number)`, the next order reuses a
  live number — would spread across arbitrary time. **None exist.**
- **A fixture-excluding predicate does not avoid the decision.** `NOT (firebase_restaurant_id LIKE
  'restaurant_test_%')` clears 279 and still fails on the 3. It would also exempt 1314 of 2600 rows
  permanently and bake a fixture-naming convention into a production index.
