## The answer in one line

**Neither.** The 876 are not QR card orders. They are stress-test fixtures, they carry N$0.00
between them, and the reason nobody noticed is that there is nothing there to notice.

## 1. What the 876 actually are

Every one of the 876 shares these properties, with no exceptions:

| | |
|---|---|
| `restaurant_id` | **NULL** — 876 of 876 |
| `firebase_restaurant_id` | `restaurant_test_02` … `restaurant_test_10`, ~98 rows each |
| `total` | **0** — 876 of 876 |
| `items` | `[{"id":"stress-item-N","name":"Test Item N", …}]`, exactly 2 lines each |
| `placed_at` | 2026-04-27, between 02:13:56 and 02:34:32 UTC — a 21-minute window, three rows per millisecond |
| `paid_at`, `completed_at`, `cancelled_at`, `accepted_at`, `updated_at` | **NULL on all 876** |
| gateway refs (`paycloud_transaction_id`, `payment_trans_no`, `payment_voucher_no`, `payment_reference`, `paycloud_merchant_order_no`, `payment_checkout_url`) | **none, on any of them** |
| `payments` row | **0 of 876** |
| `receipt_documents` row | **0 of 876** |
| `audit_logs` row | **0 of 876** |
| `is_closed` / `table_closed` | **true on all 876** |

They are 876 of a 1314-row block placed in that same 21-minute window on 2026-04-27 — 37.4% of every
order row on production. The other 438 are the cash half, sitting in `cash_pending | completed`.

The generator is `flashtap-stress-test.js` at the repo root: it posts against
`restaurant_test_01`…`_10` with items named `stress-item-N` / `Test Item N`.

**They are already in scope for `delete-324-orphan-orders.ts`.** #324 counted 1315 orphan rows with
`restaurant_id IS NULL`; 1314 of those match `restaurant_test_%`, and all 876 are inside that set.
Nothing new needs to be decided about them — the delete is written and waiting.

### Why the state looks contradictory, and what I cannot tell you

`orders.cancelled_at` did not exist until migration `20260701140000_orders_cancelled_at.sql` —
**2026-07-01, ten weeks after these rows were written.** So the absence of `cancelled_at` on all 876
is expected, not evidence of a writer that skipped it. Same for `cancellation_reason` and
`updated_at`.

Which April-2026 code path produced `cancelled | completed` on them I cannot establish, and I am not
going to guess: every column that would date the write is null, there is no audit row, and the app at
that commit is four months of history back. What I *can* say is that no current writer does it —
`expireHostedPendingOrders` sets `status='cancelled'` alongside `payment_status='cancelled'`, so it
cannot produce this pair — and that whatever did it operated on rows worth nothing, at restaurants
that do not exist.

### The control that matters

**POS card orders in the same `cancelled | completed` state: 0 of 2120.** The state is confined to
the fixture block. It is not a live pattern that the fixtures happen to be part of.

## 3. The seven that took over an hour

Not the hosted checkout being slow, and not a failure recovering late. **It is Close Table**, and the
timestamp it wrote is not a measurement of anything.

### (a) FNB ChowNow — 4 orders. The money was on time; the row was stamped late.

Each has its own `payments` row: `method=card`, `status=completed`, a distinct `gateway_reference`,
created **0.8 to 2.5 minutes** after the order was placed.

| order | placed | gateway completed | order stamped `paid_at` | apparent "wait" | real gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| #2 | 06:55:39 | 06:58:07 | 09:04:37 | 129.0 min | **2.5 min** |
| #3 | 07:01:37 | 07:02:23 | 09:04:37 | 123.0 min | **0.8 min** |
| #17 | 12:38:47 | 12:39:36 | 13:55:33 | 76.8 min | **0.8 min** |
| #18 | 12:44:41 | 12:45:36 | 13:55:33 | 70.9 min | **0.9 min** |

The stamps land in batches, to the millisecond, and `paid_at == completed_at` on every one. The
09:04:37 batch covers **#2, #3 and a CASH order #4, across three different tabs**; the 12:17:13 batch
likewise mixes card and cash across two tabs. Not one tab settling — one *table* closing.

That is the pre-fix `POST /api/tables/[tableNumber]/close`, which bulk-wrote
`status='completed', payment_status='paid', paid_at=now, completed_at=now` on every order matching
only `(restaurant_id, table_number, is_closed=false)`, with no payment guard. Fixed **2026-07-30** in
`50f826f6` — *"fix(tables): stop Close Table fabricating payment on unpaid orders"*.

**One honest limit.** That route overwrote `paid_at` unconditionally, so I cannot tell from the
orders table whether these ten were marked paid at settlement and then re-stamped, or were never
marked and only *became* paid at table close. Either way the conclusion for the ceiling work is the
same: `paid_at` on these rows is a housekeeping timestamp, and the hour it implies is dashboard lag,
not customer lag.

### (b) Digi Cofee — 3 orders. No evidence of payment at all, and one with evidence of failure.

`#1` N$3, `#2` N$3, `#6` N$30. Placed 2026-07-02. All three stamped paid at **2026-07-17 08:30:23** —
**14.8 days later**, in one instant, by the same pre-fix Close Table sweep.

- no `payments` row
- no `payment_events` row
- no gateway reference of any kind
- no receipt
- **`#1` carries an `payment.failed` audit entry at 2026-07-02 12:41:01** — three minutes after it was
  placed, reference `FT-FAIL-1782996060769`

Three orders written off as card revenue with nothing behind them, one of them after an explicit
failure. This is `50f826f6`'s defect caught in the wild, on production. **N$36, on a test account, so
no real money is missing** — but the mechanism is evidenced rather than theorised.

## 4. A correction to yesterday's customer-wait measurement

`measure-customer-wait-20260825.ts` reported *"only 43 of 1358 QR orders carry a session_id"* and
concluded the re-place scan was blind because session ids are mostly absent.

1314 of that 1358 are fixtures carrying `session_id = ''`. On the real population:

| | fixtures included | fixtures excluded |
|---|---|---|
| QR orders | 1358 | **44** |
| carrying a session_id | 43 (3.2%) | **43 (97.7%)** |
| sessions holding >1 order | 1 | 1 |

**Session ids are not the problem.** 97.7% of real QR orders carry one. The scan was blind because
production has forty-four QR orders in total and exactly one session with more than one order
(Riviera, 11 orders).

The conclusion — *not measurable* — stands. The reason given for it was wrong, and it points
somewhere different: no amount of instrumentation will produce a customer-side measurement until the
QR path has traffic. The parking decision on the customer-side ceiling is right, and this is the
stronger argument for it.

## What this changes

1. **Nothing is broken in the QR card path, and nothing was served for free.** The 876 are debris.
2. **Any share computed against the raw `orders` table is wrong by 37.4%.** The fixture block
   manufactured a 98% failure rate here; against a different filter it would just as happily
   manufacture a 98% success rate. Every future measurement needs the
   `restaurant_id IS NULL AND firebase_restaurant_id LIKE 'restaurant_test_%'` exclusion, or #324's
   delete needs to run.
3. **`50f826f6` mattered more than the commit message claims.** It is not a hypothetical: seven
   orders on production carry a fabricated or overwritten `paid_at` because of it, and three of them
   have no payment behind them at all.
4. **The customer-side ceiling stays parked, for a better reason than the one recorded.**
